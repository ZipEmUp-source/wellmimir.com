The Ward

It asks before it acts

An AI that can run commands should never do it behind your back. Before Mimir runs a command, looks at your screen or moves your mouse, it stops at your operating system's own dialog, and the answer is No until you say Yes.

  • The dialog belongs to Windows or macOS, not to a web page, and No is the default.
  • Turn a switch on once, and Mimir stops asking for that kind of action. Every switch starts off.
  • API keys are sealed by Windows or the macOS Keychain, never kept in plain text.
  • Your deny-list always wins, even when Mimir works on its own.

Your chats, on every computer

Sign in once and your chats, memory and skill notes follow you between Windows and Mac. Everything already on your computer stays right where it is.

  1. No password. Type your email, then the eight-digit code Mimir sends you.
  2. Locked before it leaves. Everything is sealed on your computer with a key only your computers hold. The account service carries it and cannot read a word.
  3. A new computer is let in by one you already use. It shows a code, and you type that code where you are already signed in.
  4. A recovery key for the rare day when none of your computers is at hand.

Questions

Asked at the Well

Is Mimir free?

Yes. Mimir is free to download and use. You pay your AI provider directly, or use the Claude or ChatGPT plan you already have.

What do I need to start?

A Windows 10 or 11 PC, or a Mac. Then one way to reach an AI:

a Claude plan through the official Claude Code app, a ChatGPT plan through the official Codex app, an API key from Anthropic, OpenAI or DeepSeek, or a model you run yourself with Ollama or any OpenAI-compatible server.

Can anyone read my chats?

Your chats live on your computer, and your messages go straight from your computer to the AI provider you picked.

With a Mimir account, what syncs between your computers is sealed on your computer first. The account service stores it but cannot read it.

Is it safe to let an AI run commands?

Mimir asks first. Commands, looks at your screen and control of your mouse and keyboard each stop at a system dialog that defaults to No, and every one of those switches starts off.

How do updates reach me?

Mimir checks for a new version a few seconds after it starts, then every six hours. When one is out, a gold pill lights up in the status bar with the release notes and an Install button.

Do I need an account?

From the next update, Mimir asks you to make a free account the first time it opens: your email and a code, no password. The account is what carries your chats between computers.

If the account service ever cannot be reached, Mimir lets you in anyway and asks again the next time it opens.

Something broke. Where do I say so?

Open an issue on GitHub and say what you did and what you saw. A screenshot helps.
